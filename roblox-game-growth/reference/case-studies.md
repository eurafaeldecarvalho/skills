# Case studies (2023-2026 hits) — what they had in common

All [CASE]: one game's story from interviews and press, not a recipe. CCU = concurrent users; "visits"
counts sessions, not unique players. Numbers as reported by the cited source; unverified figures are marked.

| Game | Launch | Reported scale | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grow a Garden** (Jandel) | Mar 2025 | ~500 → ~100k CCU in ~2 weeks after live updates; peak ~22M CCU (Aug 2025); helped push Roblox past 100M DAU | GamesBeat interview; Reuters (Jul 2025) |
| **Dead Rails** | early 2025 | hundreds → 100k+ CCU, then a ~600k weekend; "9.5M → 28M daily visits in 8 days" (**unverified**) | maxpowergaming (Mar 2025) |
| **99 Nights in the Forest** | Jun 2025 | peak ~14M CCU; built in ~3 months | PC Gamer; DevForum Creator Spotlight |
| **Scary Shawarma Kiosk: the ANOMALY** (solo dev, Yakutsk) | Oct 28 2025 | ~192k CCU (Dec 2025, Rolimon's); 1B visits by Apr 2026 | e1.ru interview (Apr 2026) |
| **DOORS** (LSPLASH) | 2022 | 3B+ visits | PCGamesN, Pocket Tactics |
| **Dress to Impress** (started by a 14-year-old dev) | Nov 2023 | > 1.7M CCU | Polygon, Vogue, Wikipedia |

## Grow a Garden
- Simple, readable loop (plant → wait → harvest → sell → upgrade), **offline growth** (a reason to come back),
  **rare/limited seeds and weather events** (urgency + clips), **trading** (social), weekly Friday updates.
- Lesson: the spike came *after* frequent live updates, not at launch; retention mechanics (come back
  tomorrow) plus regular novelty fed RFY's explore → expand.

## Dead Rails
- **Familiar base + one fresh twist** (journey/vehicle game like *a dusty trip*, but Wild West + zombies + a train),
  "MAYA" (most advanced yet acceptable). Roblox's own social accounts featured it, then large YouTubers.
- Lesson: a clear one-line premise that creators can explain in a title.

## 99 Nights in the Forest
- Mixed trends ("survive N days", Dead Rails-style danger) with a cozy forest look; **co-op with friends**
  (strongest on weekends); weekly updates with a pre-update "Update Party" event.
- Lesson: make the update itself an event that brings friends back at the same time.

## Scary Shawarma Kiosk: the ANOMALY (closest to small horror/job games)
- Began as one story inside the dev's earlier anthology game ("Short creepy stories", ~8k peak CCU), then
  became standalone — **test ideas inside an existing game** before committing.
- Loop: work a kiosk shift, follow **rules** (don't look at a certain monster, check the cameras), spot anomalies.
- A distinctive **regional vibe** (post-Soviet everyday setting) that foreign players found fresh.
- Released early because a copycat with identical rules appeared just before Halloween; later Roblox contacted
  the dev, verified the account and assigned staff who helped run weekly updates.
- Lesson: specific cultural flavour + a rules/anomaly loop + Halloween timing; speed matters when a format is hot.

## DOORS
- Procedural rooms + memorable monsters + jump scares = endless reaction content; trailers and big streamers.
- Lesson: horror spreads through **faces reacting**; design the scare to be readable on camera.

## Dress to Impress
- Social judging loop (dress to a theme, vote), meme-able moments, ties to real-world fashion trends;
  spread on TikTok, later brand collaborations.
- Lesson: if players can make content *from* the game (outfits, poses), they market it for you.

## Patterns across the hits [HEURISTIC]
1. One-line premise, familiar base, one novel twist. 2. Best with friends. 3. A reason to return tomorrow.
4. Clip-able moments in the first minutes. 5. Weekly (often Friday/pre-weekend) updates or events.
6. Timing with trends and seasons. 7. Outside attention (creators, Roblox's own socials) **after** the game retained.
